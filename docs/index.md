# Epistemic Amputation

**James Siyuan He**

[Permanent archive: doi:10.5281/zenodo.22233258](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22233258)

## The question

Can a learned belief-like representation survive the exact evidence that was
supposed to defeat it—and continue controlling a model even after the model says
the claim is false?

## What we found

The preregistered Gemma 4/J-space experiment returned **NOT SUPPORTED**.

- The model explicitly corrected its empirical answer in all 16 decisive-
  evidence cases.
- A J-space lens still detected an old-belief-like pattern in all 16 cases.
- Directly changing that suspected state changed the answer in 0/16 cases under
  the frozen causal thresholds.

The interesting result is the gap between **reading** and **causing**. A model
can contain a decodable pattern that resembles a hidden belief without that
pattern being the mechanism controlling its decision.

## Why it matters

Interpretability tools are often described as revealing what a model “really
believes.” This study supplies a stricter rule: a readable representation should
not be called a hidden belief unless controlled intervention shows that it
causes the relevant prediction or action.
